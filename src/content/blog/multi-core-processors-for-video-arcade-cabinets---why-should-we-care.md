Lots of our customers ask about the CPU performance of new Video Arcades like [DreamAuthentics Arcade Cabinets](https://dreamauthentics.com/).**It is actually a misconception that "faster is better" for any and all games. That is actually NOT TRUE for today's Interactive Kiosk cabinets such as ours.Many of the old-school "retro" games like centipede, pac-man and missile command were originally based on MOS Technology (or Metal Oxide semiconductor) which at the time ran at a blistering 1x MOS Technology 6502 @ 1.512 MHzCompare that with today's Multi Core processors (below copied from wiki) which means...A multi-core** processor (or **chip-level multiprocessor**, **CMP**) combines two or more independent **cores** (normally a [CPU](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPU)) into a single package composed of a single [integrated circuit](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_circuit) (IC), called a [die](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_%28integrated_circuit%29), or more dies packaged together. A **dual-core** processor contains two cores, and a **quad-core** processor contains four cores. A multi-core microprocessor implements [multiprocessing](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiprocessing) in a single physical package. A processor with all cores on a single die is called a **monolithic processor**. Cores in a multicore device may share a single coherent [cache](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cache) at the highest on-device cache level (e.g. L2 for the [Intel](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel_Corporation) Core 2) or may have separate caches (e.g. current [AMD](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Micro_Devices) dual-core processors). The processors also share the same interconnect to the rest of the system. Each "core" independently implements optimizations such as [superscalar](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superscalar) execution, [pipelining](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipeline_%28computing%29), and [multithreading](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multithreading_%28computer_hardware%29). A system with *n* cores is effective when it is presented with *n* or more [threads](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thread_%28computer_science%29) concurrently. The most commercially significant (or at least the most 'obvious') multi-core processors are those used in [personal computers](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_computer) (primarily from Intel and AMD) and game consoles (*e.g.*, the eight-core [Cell](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_microprocessor) processor in the [PS3](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PS3) and the three-core [Xenon](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenon_%28processor%29) processor in the [Xbox 360](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_360)). In this context, "multi" typically means a relatively small number of cores. However, the technology is widely used in other technology areas, especially those of [embedded processors](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embedded_processor), such as [network processors](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_processors) and [digital signal processors](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_signal_processors), and in [GPUs](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphics_Processing_Unit).

To play the "old school" games which essentially ran around a 1.5Mhz CPU clock cycle (equivalent to old DOS PC's) you DO NOT NEED a multi-core game engine.

HOWEVER, in the blog to follow - you WILL SEE what things to consider if you want to play some of the NEWER games which require more horsepower and if you want to do other functions on your DreamAuthentics game such as video editing and more processor intensive tasks.
